Problem Statement

Mutual Exclusion

Delta Corp is evaluating two mutually exclusive machines. Machine Alpha costs $60,000, incurs annual operating costs of $8,000, and lasts 3 years with zero salvage value. Machine Beta costs $85,000, incurs annual operating costs of $6,000, and lasts 5 years with $5,000 salvage. Delta's cost of capital is 10%. Using the Equivalent Annual Cost (EAC) method, determine which machine should be selected.

Given Parameters r = 10% (0.10)
Alpha n = 3 yrs
Outlay (C₀):-$60,000
Op. Cost:-$8,000/yr
Salvage (SV):$0
Beta n = 5 yrs
Outlay (C₀):-$85,000
Op. Cost:-$6,000/yr
Salvage (SV):+$5,000
functions Core Valuation Methodology
EAC = Present Value of Total Costs ÷ PVIFA(r, n)
PVIFA(r, n) [ 1 − (1 + r)-n ] ÷ r

Step-by-Step Derivation

5 Milestones
1

Present Value of Alpha's Costs

Discount the 3-year operating annuity and aggregate with the initial capital outlay.

PVIFA(10%, 3 yrs) [1 − (1.10)-3] / 0.10 = 2.48685
PV of Operating Costs $8,000 × 2.48685 = $19,894.80
Total PV of Costs (Alpha) $60,000 + $19,894.80 = $79,894.80
lightbulb

Examiner Tip: Do not treat initial capital outlay as a cash flow to be discounted; it is already committed at t = 0.

2

Equivalent Annual Cost (Alpha)

Distribute total PV over the 3-year useful lifespan annuity factor.

Calculation: $79,894.80 ÷ 2.48685
EAC (Machine Alpha) $32,126.86 / yr
3

Present Value of Beta's Costs

Aggregate 5-year operating annuity and capital outlay, subtracting terminal salvage inflow.

PVIFA(10%, 5 yrs) [1 − (1.10)-5] / 0.10 = 3.79079
PV of Operating Costs $6,000 × 3.79079 = $22,744.74
PV Salvage Value (Year 5) $5,000 × (1.10)-5 = $5,000 × 0.62092
− $3,104.60
Net Total PV (Beta) $85k + $22.7k − $3.1k = $104,640.14
verified

Salvage Treatment: Salvage acts as a cash recovery at the end of Year 5. In a cost-minimization setup, it is strictly deducted from cumulative PV costs.

4

Equivalent Annual Cost (Beta)

Convert net PV over Beta's 5-year annuity factor.

Calculation: $104,640.14 ÷ 3.79079
EAC (Machine Beta) $27,603.78 / yr
check
Step 5: Executive Conclusion
Selected
Select Machine Beta Save $4,523.08/yr

Although Machine Beta demands a higher capital commitment upfront ($85,000 vs. $60,000), its longer life and lower operating costs yield an Equivalent Annual Cost of $27,603.78 compared to $32,126.86 for Machine Alpha.

Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) Cost of Capital
WACC = (E/V)·Kₑ + (D/V)·K_d·(1 - T) + (P/V)·K_p

Note: Always apply target market value weights, not historical balance sheet book values.

Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM) Equity
Kₑ = R_f + β × (R_m - R_f)

Where (R_m - R_f) is the Equity Risk Premium (ERP). Beta measures systematic market risk.

Equivalent Annual Cost (EAC) Capital Budgeting
EAC = Net PV of Lifecycle Costs ÷ PVIFA(r, n)

Used to evaluate mutually exclusive assets with mismatched productive lifespans.

Bond YTM Approximation Formula Fixed Income
YTM ≈ [ C + (F - P) / n ] ÷ [ (F + P) / 2 ]

For semi-annual coupons: replace C with C/2, replace n with 2n, and double the periodic yield.

warning Top 5 Candidate Traps & University Pitfalls
1. Terminal Value Timing in DCF

In explicit multi-year models, numerator for terminal value is CF_{n+1} = CF_n × (1 + g). Never use CF_n without the growth increment! Also remember to discount TV back to t = 0.

2. Salvage Value Deduction in EAC

Salvage value is a cash inflow at the end of the machine's life. In EAC cost minimization, deduct its discounted present value from total costs before dividing by PVIFA.

3. Flotation Costs Adjustment Location

Flotation costs should be added to the initial investment outlay at t = 0: Outlay / (1 - f). Adjusting WACC creates an artificial perpetual penalty.

4. Semi-Annual Coupon Period Doubling

Always multiply lifespan n by 2 and divide annual coupon by 2. Forgetting to double n is the most common time-pressure exam mistake.
